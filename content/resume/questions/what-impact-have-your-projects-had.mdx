title: What's your impact across these projects? Many are prototypes with no users—why keep building them, and why not emphasize them on your résumé?
order: 5
answer: |-
  In short, I do not treat impact as the primary reason to work on something.

  People often evaluate a project by how many users it reached, how many people it helped, or what measurable social change it produced. Those can be useful observations, but I am skeptical of treating them as a universal evaluation function. I also reject the claim that something should be done simply because it is “right.” Rightness is not a neutral, universally shared quantity; in practice, it usually represents a particular person's interests, values, or evaluation function.

  I choose projects mainly from personal interest and from the kind of person I want to become: someone I myself would want as a friend—curious, technically capable, independent, willing to care, and willing to help without requiring recognition. That standard is deliberately independent of other people's judgment in most cases.

  This is also why I avoid overstating my contribution. Some projects helped classmates, club members, or teammates, but in many cases my individual effect was small. Someone else with a similar background could have provided comparable help. I helped because I cared about the people or the problem, not because I wanted attention, gratitude, or confirmation that others shared my priorities. Describing that as “doing the right thing” would obscure the actual reason.

  The prototypes with zero users still matter to me. They are experiments: ways to test an idea, expose gaps in my understanding, and acquire hands-on experience. A prototype can fail as a product and still succeed as a learning instrument. It belongs in my public project record because that record preserves what I attempted and learned.

  A résumé has a different purpose and a severe information constraint. I therefore select projects that provide the clearest evidence for a particular role rather than listing every repository or implying that every prototype created external impact. The broader website preserves the rest for anyone who wants the context. The résumé is a filtered interface, not a complete moral accounting of my work.
